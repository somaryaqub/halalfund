01
The Foundation

Why Housing —
the case we stand on.

Housing is not a new direction for IslamicFamily. It is, in many ways, the original wound — the need that has been present in our community since the beginning, that we have been working toward for over a decade. Our communication must always start here: with the families we know, and with the research that proves what they have always told us.

The Human Reality
Sarah

A mother in an abusive situation caring for both her children and her parents. Shelters won't accept her whole family. The choice she is given: leave her parents, abandon her kids, or stay. This is not a choice.

Adam

Because of war, he now cares for his nephews, nieces, and his own children — a family of twelve. There is no apartment large enough. After all that hardship, they cannot even be together under one roof.

A grandmother's family

A grandmother, her daughter, and four grandchildren shared 700 sq ft for eleven months. Three children slept in the living room. Their caseworker: "There is simply nothing available at the size they need."

The Research Foundation
The Halal Housing Lab (2021–2023) · National Award-Winning Research

A two-year CMHC Solutions Lab investigation that won both the Alberta Institute of Planners and Canadian Institute of Planners awards. It produced the fund structure, architectural model, financing framework, and partnership logic that makes this project possible. The resulting open-source toolkit — halalhousinglab.ca — documents 15 building blocks for culturally appropriate Muslim housing. Core finding: Canadian housing policy structurally discriminates against larger and extended families, and halal-compliant community housing financing has never existed in Canada — until now.

Effective Zakat in Canada (2022) · share.islamicfamily.ca/effectivezakat

Co-developed with Dr. Katherine Bullock and reviewed by Islamic finance scholars, this paper establishes that piecemeal, transactional zakat — emergency food hampers, small one-time payments — does not disrupt the structural poverty it is meant to address. Families come back to our door year after year with the same unresolved needs. This research is the foundation for our zakat-as-rent-supplement model: using community generosity at a systemic level, not just a symptomatic one. It is also the basis for our alignment with MAC's Zakat House and the Canadian Council of Imams.

04
Our Approach

How we did this —
innovation that protected what existed.

The most important communication point about how this happened: we did not take money from existing programs. We used innovation infrastructure — specifically designed for new, risky, exploratory work — to build something new.

Condition 1 CMHC Solutions Lab Funding — Innovation Capital, Not Program Dollars

The Halal Housing Lab was funded by Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation's NHS Solutions Labs program — innovation grants specifically designed for exploratory, high-risk research. Not a single dollar of IslamicFamily's charitable programs or zakat funds was diverted to this work. The research won both the Alberta Institute of Planners Award and the Canadian Institute of Planners Award.

Condition 2 Strategic Partnerships — Expertise We Didn't Have to Build From Scratch

Rather than building every capability from zero, we assembled a team of values-aligned partners:

  • SVX Canada — Fund structure, investor relations, securities compliance. $60M+ in managed assets, $500M+ mobilized.
  • Vivid Homes / Build Edmonton Homes — Self-financed developer, halal-compliant. No interest-bearing construction debt. Their CEO Issam Saleh has been an IslamicFamily partner for over a decade.
  • Pallett Valo LLP — Legal counsel for the LP/GP structure and securities compliance.
  • Mufti Zain Baig — Independent Shariah compliance review.
  • City of Edmonton + Province of Alberta — $11.1M in confirmed anchor capital (land, municipal grants, provincial grants). 30% of the total project cost secured before a single community investor dollar is committed.
Condition 3 Structural Separation — IslamicFamily's Programs Are Fully Protected

The Halal Affordable Housing Investment Fund (HAHIF) is a legally separate Limited Partnership. IslamicFamily is its operator and an equity holder — not a financial guarantor:

  • No general operating funds of IslamicFamily are pledged, co-signed, or at risk at any stage.
  • Counselling, family support, the food bank, newcomer settlement — none of these are affected, reduced, or compromised.
  • The fund is financed by impact investors, government grants, and zakat rent supplements — not by IslamicFamily's existing charitable capital.
05
Objection Handling

Addressing concerns —
what people will ask.

These are the concerns that will surface from staff, volunteers, donors, community members, and board members. Every person on this team should be able to answer these clearly and with confidence. The answers are honest, not defensive.

Will this take money away from counselling, the food bank, or other existing programs?

No — and it will improve them. The housing fund is a legally separate entity from IslamicFamily's general operating budget. The fund is capitalized by impact investors and government grants, not from IslamicFamily's charitable programs or zakat distribution.

More importantly: when families have stable housing, they need fewer counselling sessions, less emergency food support, and less crisis intervention. Housing doesn't compete with these programs — it makes them more effective.

Will this pull focus and management time away from other areas of our work?

In the short term, it helps offset program and administrative costs — and builds organizational resilience. Once operational, Diamond at Dunluce will generate sustainable revenue for IslamicFamily through property management — reducing our dependence on the grant cycle that constrains every other program we run.

Yes, the ED and senior leadership will invest real time in this project between now and 2028. That is acknowledged and planned for. The commitment is that existing program obligations will be maintained.

Is this actually halal? Who verified it?

Yes — independently reviewed and certified. Mufti Zain Baig, an independent Islamic finance scholar (not employed by IslamicFamily or SVX), has reviewed and certified the fund's LP/GP structure, the Musharakah framework, the investor return structure, and the absence of any interest-bearing instruments. This is not a workaround. The fund uses no riba. Returns come from shared equity participation in a housing asset — not interest on money.

What if the project runs into trouble? Is IslamicFamily on the hook?

IslamicFamily's finances are protected at every stage. The fund's legal structure separates IslamicFamily's organizational finances from the project's financial obligations. If the project underperforms, investor capital may be affected — investors enter knowing and accepting this risk — but IslamicFamily's operating budget, programs, and staff are insulated. Multiple Board-approval gates exist before any major commitment is made.

Is this only for Muslim families? Are we prioritizing some people over others?

No. Tenancy is not restricted by religion. The fund's investment criteria prioritize larger family-sized, affordable units for underserved households. The tenant population will include Muslim and non-Muslim families who qualify under affordability criteria. Muslim families are more than twice as likely to be in core housing need as the average Canadian — responding to that documented, data-grounded need is equitable design, not preferential treatment.

Why now? Why not wait until we have more resources?

Because three conditions that have never simultaneously existed before are open right now — and each has a closing window. For the first time in our 35-year history, we have a viable halal finance structure, a self-financed culturally aligned builder, and $11.1M in confirmed government anchor capital. Waiting does not preserve the status quo — it forecloses an opportunity that may not recur for another decade.

Will this initiative compete with or reduce donations to IslamicFamily's existing programs?

No — and this is a critical point to be clear on with every donor conversation. The Halal Affordable Housing Investment Fund is a separate financial vehicle. It draws on a completely different pool of capital: impact investors, government grants, and institutional philanthropy — not the community zakat and sadaqa that funds our counselling, food bank, settlement programs, and youth work.

A donor who gives to IslamicFamily's annual campaign is supporting programs. A donor who invests in the HAHIF is deploying capital into a structured fund — a different decision, for a different purpose, from a different part of their financial life. These are not in competition. In fact, housing stability reduces the demand on our emergency programs over time, meaning existing donations go further.

The message for donors is straightforward: your annual gift still does exactly what it has always done. The housing fund is additive — it does not replace, redirect, or diminish anything you already support.

Events

AGM or special
housing event.

The primary vehicle for housing-specific engagement is an intimate event — either the AGM or a dedicated housing gathering — held at the IslamicFamily Hüb. Approximately 50 people. The focus is institutional investors and serious prospective funders, not a broad community audience. Small enough for real conversation, significant enough to signal that this is a serious initiative at an important stage.

Guest List Principles
  • Primary guests: Institutional investors and philanthropic funders in active conversation with SVX or IslamicFamily — this is a closing event, not an introductory one.
  • Secondary guests: Community champions and board members who can speak credibly to the organization's track record and lend social proof to the room.
  • Not the right room for: Broad community members, general donors, or anyone who hasn't already been introduced to the fund. Those relationships belong in different channels.
  • Size discipline: Keep to ~50. A room of 50 serious people is more powerful than a room of 200 casual ones.
Follow-Up Protocol — 48 Hours Post-Event
  • SVX follows up directly with every guest who filled out a commitment card — within 48 hours, not 5 days
  • ED sends a personal note to every institutional guest, regardless of whether they committed — relationship maintenance matters as much as the close
  • Anyone who attended but didn't commit gets a follow-up meeting offer from SVX within two weeks
  • Event summary (attendees, commitments, pipeline status) reported to the Board within one week

Risk Communications

Obstacle & setback
protocols.

Transparency is our most important asset. When things go wrong — and in a project of this complexity, some things will — the community will forgive honest difficulty far more readily than evasion. These are the scenarios to plan for.

ScenarioRisk LevelProtocolKey Message
First close not reached by Aug 15 High Board briefed immediately. Staff within 24 hours. No public announcement yet. ED reviews options with SVX within 48 hours. "We fell short of the milestone required to proceed. IslamicFamily's finances are fully protected. We are reviewing next steps and will keep you informed. The need has not gone away."
Construction delays Medium Proactive update in investor email as soon as delay is confirmed. Social media post with revised timeline. Do not wait for community members to ask. "Diamond at Dunluce is experiencing a [X-month] delay due to [reason]. Move-in target is now [new date]. Nothing about the project's fundamentals has changed."
Cost overruns Medium Immediate notification to Board Finance Committee. Disclosure in next investor update. ED statement on mitigation steps. "Project costs have increased due to [specific cause]. Here is how this affects the capital stack and what management and SVX are doing to address it."
Community criticism or skepticism Medium Engage directly — never ignore. Invite critics to a Q&A. Publish Shariah compliance documentation publicly if the concern is about halal status. Respond once, clearly, with facts. Use the FAQ from Section 05. Be direct, not defensive. The research, the scholars, and the government support are all publicly documented.
Loss of a key partner High Immediate board notification. Internal assessment of impact before any public communication. If the project can continue, announce the change and explain the path forward. "[Partner] is no longer part of this project. Here is how we are responding." — Followed immediately by the mitigation plan.
Staff questions about program impacts Low Field through direct managers. Any question not answered by this plan escalates to ED within 24 hours. "This project does not touch existing program budgets. If you have a concern, please raise it with your manager or directly with Omar."
Move-in delays / families asking for updates Medium Proactive communication to families who have expressed interest. Honest timelines. Regular updates — even if the update is "no change." Silence is the worst response here. "Move-in is expected in [timeframe]. We will contact you directly as the process moves forward. Your place on our list is not affected."
General Setback Principles
  • Internal before external. Board and staff always hear from us before the community does.
  • Named, specific, honest. Never vague. Say what happened, why, and what we are doing about it.
  • Lead with organizational protection. In any difficult announcement, the first sentence after the news is: "IslamicFamily's existing programs and general operating funds are not affected."
  • Keep the human story central. Even in a setback, the families who need this housing are still waiting. That doesn't change, and neither does our commitment.
  • One voice externally. All media and major community communications go through the ED or a designated spokesperson. Staff should direct external inquiries to Omar.

"The community will forgive honest difficulty. It will not forgive the discovery that we knew something and didn't tell them."
