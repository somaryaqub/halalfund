About the Proponent

IslamicFamily — Three Decades of Community Service

IslamicFamily (Islamic Family and Social Services Association) is an Edmonton-based registered charity with over 30 years of service to Muslim and broader communities. The Halal Affordable Housing Investment Fund is the culmination of a generation of groundwork.

Who We Are

Founded in September 1992 and incorporated under the Societies Act of Alberta, IslamicFamily has grown from a small community association into one of Canada's most recognized nonprofits. Our programs span family counselling, food security, newcomer settlement, domestic violence support, and now affordable housing development.

We hold Imagine Canada accreditation — a rigorous national standard that fewer than 1% of Canadian charities achieve — attesting to our financial transparency, governance, and program effectiveness.

Institutional History in Housing

This fund is not a new initiative — it is the result of three decades of community need, a decade of barrier-identification, and three years of purpose-built fund design.

1992–2015
IslamicFamily incorporated. Housing identified as the underlying crisis in nearly every family intake file. Organizational capacity building begins.
2016–2019
Partnership with Right at Home housing developer advanced — then collapsed when conventional interest-bearing debt (prohibited under Shariah) was required. The financing barrier was formally identified.
2021–2023
Halal Housing Lab — funded by CMHC — brings together affordable housing, architecture, Islamic finance, and urban planning experts. Wins both a provincial and national planning award (AIP/CIP). Produces the fund structure underpinning HAHIF.
2023–2025
Board formally adopts affordable housing as a strategic priority. Vivid Homes identified as development partner. City of Edmonton awards land at Dunluce. Provincial and municipal grants secured (~$10M). SVX Canada engaged as fund management partner.
2025–2026 (Now)
Construction underway on Diamond at Dunluce. Fund documentation finalized with Pallett Valo LLP. Shariah compliance certified by Mufti Zain Baig. Active capital raise live at halalfund.ca.

Shariah Compliance

Islamic Finance at the Foundation

Shariah compliance is not an overlay on this fund — it is the reason the fund exists. Every structural element has been designed from first principles to align with Islamic finance.

لا رِبَا — No Interest. No Ambiguity.

Certified Shariah-Compliant Structure

The Halal Affordable Housing Investment Fund is reviewed and certified by Mufti Zain Baig, an independent Shariah advisor. The fund uses a Musharakah (profit-sharing) structure — investors hold proportional equity, returns derive from real asset performance, and no interest-bearing instruments are used at any level of the capital stack. This structure has been reviewed by legal counsel Pallett Valo LLP and confirmed compatible with Alberta securities regulation.

No riba (interest). All returns are profit-sharing based on real asset performance.
Asset-backed. Every unit of capital is tied to a tangible real property.
Risk-sharing. Investors and GP share in the risks and rewards proportionally.
Socially responsible. Proceeds serve affordable housing, a fard kifaya (collective duty).
No gharar. Terms, risk distribution, and return waterfall are fully transparent.
Residual to charity. Residual proceeds, after LP distributions, return to IslamicFamily for charitable purposes.

Shariah Advisor

Mufti Zain Baig serves as independent Shariah compliance advisor to the fund. He has reviewed the GP/LP structure, the Musharakah investment agreement, the LP term sheet, and the investor documentation and issued a compliance certification. Why This Project Matters

Black Muslim families in Edmonton are 16× more likely to live in subsidized housing than the general population. Muslim Canadians face a core housing need rate of 11% — vs. 4.9% nationally. The average family waits 3–4 years for an affordable family-sized unit. Diamond at Dunluce is purpose-built to change this — 264 bedrooms sized for the families the market has consistently failed.
