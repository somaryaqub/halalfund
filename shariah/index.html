I Foundation

Founded on Shariah, not retrofitted.

There is a real difference between a fund that is Shariah-compliant and one that is Shariah-based. Compliance is a screen applied to a conventional structure after the fact: take an ordinary vehicle, strip out what is prohibited, and certify what survives. A Shariah-based fund begins from the other direction. The principles come first, and the structure is built to serve them.

The Housing Prosperity Fund is Shariah-based. Its ownership, its returns, and its sharing of risk were designed around Islamic finance from the outset, not adapted to it later. Everything you will read below, the equity structure, the absence of interest-bearing debt, the treatment of tenants, exists because the fund was shaped around these commitments before it was shaped around anything else.

II Origin

Born of a decade spent refusing riba.

IslamicFamily began working on affordable housing in 2016. For the better part of a decade, the same wall stood in the way: the financing that makes affordable housing possible in Canada, the CMHC-backed programs everyone else relies on, are built on interest-bearing debt.

We walked away from deals rather than sign a conventional mortgage, even when the rate offered was below prime. A lower rate is still riba, and a project that houses Muslim families on the back of interest was not a project we were willing to build.

That refusal cost us time and more than one opportunity. It also produced the conviction, and eventually the structure, that there had to be a halal way to finance shelter at scale in this country.

Our conviction led to two years of research, the Halal Housing Lab, and now this fund. This research connected the barriers we face with conventional finance, with the opportunities to more intentionally channel community wealth transfers (zakat). The Halal Housing Lab research won a Canadian Institute of Planners' National Award, and an Alberta Professional Planners Institute (APPI) Merit Award.

III Guidance

Scholarship at the table from the beginning.

Shariah oversight on this fund is not a certificate collected at the end. It has been part of the work from the earliest stages.

Certification is handled by Mufti Mirza-Zain Baig, an AAOIFI Certified Shariah Advisor and Auditor (CSAA) who specialises in Islamic finance within the Canadian context. Working in a Canadian context matters here. The rulings that govern this fund have to sit correctly against Canadian property law, tenancy law, and the specific instruments our counsel at Pallett Valo draft. A scholar working close to those realities can review the actual contracts, not an idealised version of them.

Mufti Mirza-Zain has been engaged with the structure alongside our legal team and SVX throughout, and will review each contract as it is finalised before providing certification. The fund's design is also grounded in the contemporary scholarship that draws the line between Shariah-based and merely Shariah-compliant finance, the same distinction this document opens with.

IV Structure

Free of interest at every level.

The test of a Shariah-based fund is not the headline. It is whether interest has been kept out of every layer, including the ones investors rarely see. Here is the fund read from the top down.

Capital & ownership

A partnership, not a loan.

The fund is structured as a musharakah: a general partnership between IslamicFamily (60%) and SVX (40%), with investors participating as limited partners who own a share of the asset and share in its risk and its return. Returns come from the housing itself, from rent and the value of real property, never from interest on money lent. Class 0 units carry concessionary capital; Class 1 units seek a modest return of roughly three percent. No investor is promised a fixed rate detached from the performance of the asset, because a guaranteed return on capital is the very thing we are avoiding.

Acquisition & financing

No interest-bearing debt in the stack.

There is no riba-based leverage anywhere in the acquisition or the financing of the project, now or at any later stage. The years of walking away from conventional mortgages were not wasted: the same discipline governs the fund itself. Where the structure calls for financing beyond the initial raise, it is arranged through halal, equity-based means, and never a conventional loan.

Tenants & operations

No interest charged to the people we house.

Tenants are never charged interest, including on late rent. As a social service organisation, IslamicFamily works to prevent late payment in the first place, through financial literacy, community-based rent supplements, help accessing government programs, and the wraparound support our tenants are already connected to. Where late payment does occur, it is handled by a professional third-party manager, with a clear line between property management and social work. Compassion and professionalism, but never riba.

Contracts & disputes

The commitment reaches the paperwork.

The purchase agreement and the fund's instruments are drafted to be free of riba, gharar, and maysir, and reviewed on that basis. Even the dispute-resolution terms are written so that no interest can be charged on amounts in dispute. Shariah is a binding constraint on every document, not a principle that stops at the summary page.

✸ What this means for you

Permitted, and purposeful.

For a Muslim investor, this fund offers something rare: capital placed in a way that is not only halal but purposeful. Your investment builds homes for the larger and extended families the housing system was never designed for, beginning with the Diamond at Dunluce in Edmonton, 96 homes now under construction. It is sadaqah jariyah alongside a stable return, an act of ibadah that also does the ordinary, honourable work of sheltering people.

The fund also carries a zakat-funded rent supplement, structured so the zakat becomes the tenant's own (tamlik) through a wakalah agency arrangement, so that zakat can be deployed correctly within the model rather than bent to fit it.

This is not a conventional fund made acceptable. It is a halal structure built for a halal purpose, guided by scholarship from the beginning, and free of interest at every level. That is the standard we hold ourselves to, and the one we invite you to hold us to.
